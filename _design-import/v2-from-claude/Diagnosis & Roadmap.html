Step 01

Diagnosis

Read first · Confirm before we build

I read the artifact files — colors_and_type.css, ui-kit.css, the brand sheet — and stress-tested your eleven hypotheses against the production tokens. You're mostly right, but you've misnamed the disease. The app doesn't lack basketball-ness because it's missing court graphics. It lacks basketball-ness because nothing on screen has earned the right to be loud. Glass is a quiet material. You stacked five quiet materials and asked them to feel like a buzzer-beater.

A · Validating your 11 hypotheses

§5.1

No "what now" on any tab landing

Agree — strongest symptom

Confirmed. The hierarchy isn't broken at the component level — it's broken at the screen level. Every tab is a stack of glass cards of equal mass. The fix isn't a bigger card; it's a singular, asymmetric hero that takes 40–55% of the viewport and an explicit "demoted" zone below it. Today's first card already wants to be the hero — it's just not built like one.

§5.2

Everything is glass, nothing stands out

Agree — root cause

This is the disease. Glass is an "ambient" material in the design system's own grammar (the README literally says "Glass is for containers, not for everything"). The fix is a three-tier surface system: hero (solid accent or photo) → glass (ambient grouping) → flat (dividers, rows, lists). Right now we use one tier for all three jobs.

§5.3

Legacy sub-pages break the experience

Agree — biggest UX cliff

Confirmed and probably worse than you think. The legacy .ks-*/.db-* screens (Log/History/Alerts/Moves/Shop/Onboarding) outnumber the v2 screens. Every tab has at least one cliff. This is the #1 priority on the roadmap below — not because legacy is ugly, but because it kills the brand promise on the second tap.

§5.4

Coach feels template-y, not premium

Agree — but reframe

You're right that the bulleted-list pattern reads as "AI output, formatted." But the diagnosis is wrong. The fix isn't less structure. A real coach IS structured — they give you a verdict, the why, and the prescription. The problem is tonal: the verdict needs to feel spoken (italic display, second-person, one sentence), and the prescription needs to feel physical (one drill card the coach is "handing you," not three suggestions on a list).

Reframe: Coach isn't a chatbot transcript. Coach is a Polaroid clipped to your locker.

§5.5

Five accents fight for attention

Agree — and CSS rule isn't enough

The .ciq-accent-faded class exists but it's a band-aid. The real rule needs to be structural: only the active tab's accent is "live" (full saturation, full glow, motion-able). Cross-tab references render as text-only token references — never as a colored card. When Coach recommends a Train drill, the recommendation card stays purple-accented (Coach is the speaker), and only an arrow + the drill name point to Train. We don't render a blue card inside Coach.

§5.6

Inconsistent empty / loading states

Agree

Real, but lower priority. The fix is mechanical: enforce .ciq-empty / .ciq-loading as the only legal templates and add the four missing states (error, success, skeleton, locked) — see §New Tokens.

§5.7

No basketball-native character

Half right — see thesis below

You're right that swapping icons isn't enough. You're also right that "rhythm, density, information cadence" matters. But you're missing one specific thing — see the dedicated thesis block below. Short version: basketball is a court of measured zones, not a feed of cards, and the app should structurally inherit that.

§5.8

Missing micro-interactions, no celebrations

Agree — but pick ONE signature

Confirmed. The trap to avoid: building five different celebrations (made-shot, streak, badge, level-up, workout-complete) all in different motion vocabularies. One signature gesture — the "swish" — applied at five intensities. See §Motion Budget.

§5.9

Camera HUD looks like a debug overlay

Agree — and it's the demo

This is the feature you'd put in the App Store screenshot. Right now it looks like a Chrome devtools panel. Treatment direction: broadcast scoreboard, not telemetry. Score is biggest. % is hero stat. Status is a single pulsing dot, not three system messages. Detection boxes are debug-only and toggle off by default in production.

§5.10

Avatar & Shop feel disconnected

Agree

Avatar should be a first-class identity surface, not a settings input. It should appear at four scales (16/24/48/300px), animate when XP is earned, and be the visual anchor of every leaderboard row. Shop should look like a 2K MyTeam pack, not a settings list — see roadmap row §6.13.

§5.11

Onboarding doesn't set the tone

Agree — and we should rebuild this LAST, not first

You called it: this is the user's first impression. But here's the counterintuitive move — we redesign onboarding last. Why? Because the scouting-report reveal at step 7 should hand the user into the app's strongest screens. We can't design the cliff until we've built the landing.

B · Three things you missed

+1

The brand color from the logo isn't in the token system

Net new

The brand sheet is unmistakably red-orange (#FF1A00) — basketball leather, the iq mark, the targeting crosshair, all of it. The codebase has --c-amber #f5a623 as "brand primary" — but amber is a Home tab accent, not a brand color. They are doing different jobs and right now they collide. Proposal: introduce --c-brand: #ff3a14 as the marketing/chrome/logo color, leave amber as Home tab accent. See new tokens below.

+2

The 96-token system has no "depth" tokens — only "color" tokens

Net new

Looking at colors_and_type.css: there are 5 surface levels but no semantic elevation tokens. A "hero card" and a "list row" both reach into --glass-bg. That's why everything visually flattens. Proposal: add --surface-hero, --surface-glass, --surface-flat as semantic surface roles that map to existing values but lock them by job.

+3

The "italic display" type is doing too much work alone

Net new

Italic Barlow Condensed is the single strongest brand signal in the app. But it's used everywhere — H1, H2, big stats, eyebrows. When a signature is everywhere, it's nowhere. Proposal: italic = "the human voice" only (verdicts, hero titles, celebration overlays). Stats and labels stay upright. This gives the italic moments emotional weight again.

C · The three highest-leverage problems

If we solve these three, every other symptom in §5 collapses or shrinks. Sequence the entire roadmap around them.

Priority 01
Define the surface trinity

Hero · Glass · Flat. One semantic ladder, three jobs. Once this lands, "no hero on landings," "glass everywhere," and "five accents fighting" all dissolve. This is a token-and-CSS week, not a screen week — and it must come first.

Priority 02
Port every legacy sub-page in one wave

Don't drip the legacy fix across tabs. One wave: kill .ks-* / .db-* entirely by porting Log, History, Alerts, Moves, Shop, and Onboarding behind feature flags. The cliff isn't acceptable in any tab.

Priority 03
Make Track the showcase screen first

Track is the flagship — both Lab landing and the camera HUD. If we rebuild Track to look like ESPN broadcast, the app's quality bar resets. Every other tab gets measured against it. Build Track-Lab + Camera HUD before touching Coach or Me polish.

D · The basketball question

My thesis · Argue back if it's wrong
A basketball app feels like basketball when its information lives where the action lives — on the court, not in cards stacked off it.

Basketball isn't a list. It's a half-court diagram with seven zones, each carrying meaning: corner threes, the wing, the elbow, the paint, the restricted area. The half-court is the primary information architecture of this app, not the bottom nav. Stats hung on zones, drills sorted by zone, history filtered by zone, the camera calibrated against zones, the avatar's "specialty" expressed as a zone heatmap. The court is the layout grid — and the brand's circuit-line aesthetic is what the zone outlines should look like (technical, vector-precise, not painted).

Apple Sports, Strava, NTC don't get to use this. We do. The half-court diagram is our killer layout — not just a Track-tab visualization. When that thesis is wrong, the design will tell you. When it's right, the app will feel unmistakably like basketball with no extra ornament.

New tokens · icons · templates

New tokens
  • --c-brand#ff3a14 · Brand red-orange, lifted from the logo. Logo, splash, error/celebration accents, app icon background. Distinct from amber.
  • --surface-heroSolid accent or photo. One per screen. Replaces ad-hoc --c-surface3 reaches.
  • --surface-glassAliases --glass-bg. Legal only inside grouped content.
  • --surface-flatTransparent. List rows, dividers, inline content.
  • --c-errorAliases --c-brand. Camera permission denied, AI failures, network errors.
  • --c-successAliases --c-track green. Workout complete, session saved.
  • --ease-swishcubic-bezier(.2,.85,.3,1) · The signature easing. Made-shot flashes, level-ups.

The basketball move · One concrete decision

The half-court SVG isn't a Track-tab visualization. It's the app's primary information surface.

One concrete commitment: every tab references the same half-court SVG component at one of three scales — full (Track Lab), thumbnail (Home recent sessions, Me profile), glyph (16px inline references). When a Coach insight references "your right-corner three," the corner zone glows at 16px inside Coach's text. When a leaderboard row shows a player's specialty, it's a 24px half-court mini-heatmap. The basketball-ness is structural, not decorative.
